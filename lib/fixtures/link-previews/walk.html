The Weekender

A slow walk along Regent's Canal

By Mara Ellis

Start at Little Venice, where the narrowboats sit two deep and the water is so still it holds the white stucco houses upside down. There is no hurry here, and that is the point: the towpath runs for nearly nine miles to Limehouse Basin, and you can walk it in an afternoon if you keep stopping.

Past the zoo the canal drops into a cutting, and the city goes quiet. You can hear the aviary before you see it. Joggers pass in ones and twos; a heron stands on the far bank and does not move for the whole time it takes to finish a coffee.

At Camden the locks are the busiest part of the walk. The lock keepers will tell you which boats are on their way through and how long the wait will be, and most afternoons there is a small crowd watching the gates open as if it were theatre. Go round the market rather than through it.

From King's Cross the canal changes again. Granary Square has its steps down to the water, and further on, the gasholders frame new flats in iron lace. The towpath narrows under the bridges, so take the corners slowly and ring if you are on a bike.

The last stretch through Victoria Park is the greenest. By the time you reach Limehouse the light is going and the basin is full of masts. Sit for a while. There is a pub on the corner that has been there longer than the canal.
